### 3.3 The Amlodipine Reflex Tachycardia (resting 85, exertional 110–116) — the "HR 185" was a typo for 85

**Clinical facts — CORRECTED (2026-08-05).** On switching to Amlotel (amlodipine 10 mg + telmisartan 80 mg), BP fell to ~120 and heart rate rose to **85 at rest** (smartwatch 85–91 resting / 110–116 on stairs/attic — the normal 10–25 bpm dihydropyridine reflex add), with an audible heartbeat; then three nights of stomach pain/vomiting; Dr. Budi Gunawan stopped the amlodipine. **The "185" in the original record was a digit typo for 85 (corrected 2026-08-05 in COMPLICATIONS.md): there was no resting 185 and no "heart-rate storm."** This section is retained (rather than deleted) for the correction trail; the analysis below is re-read against the corrected numbers.

**Analysis — pharmacology first (and none needed for the corrected numbers).**

1. **[ESTABLISHED] High-dose dihydropyridine reflex tachycardia.** Amlodipine 10 mg is a maximum antihypertensive dose; arterial vasodilation triggers reflex sympathetic activation. A resting HR of 85 rising to 110–116 on exertion is **exactly the expected response** — the standard 10–25 bpm reflex add of a high-dose dihydropyridine — not an extreme manifestation.
2. **[ESTABLISHED — as a general hazard, not as an explanation for this episode] Abrupt beta-blocker withdrawal.** Atenolol was discontinued the same day the Amlotel was started. Sudden beta-blocker withdrawal causes sympathetic rebound (upregulation of beta-receptors) and can cause severe tachycardia or arrhythmia — so **any future switch off nebivolol must be tapered** — but the withdrawal-rebound amplifier is no longer needed to explain an extreme rate, because no extreme rate occurred.
3. **[MODERATE] Stomach pain and vomiting** — recognized amlodipine/ARB-side-effect overlap (dyspepsia is common with telmisartan; amlodipine can cause GI upset); the genetics do not speak to this.

**What the genetics add (or fail to add).**

- **Tested beta-receptor SNPs were graded neutral**: ADRB2 rs1042714 CC, rs1042713 AA ("no impact") and ADRB3 rs4994 TC (moderate, in the weight section). Consistent with the corrected picture — a normal reflex response needs no beta-receptor exaggeration.
- **COMT rs4680 GG (high)** — catecholamine clearance. The report itself is internally contradictory about this variant (§5): it claims both "efficient estrogen metabolism / low estrogen / low dopamine" and, in the performance section, that Val/Val means "lower COMT enzyme activity and higher catecholamine (fight-or-flight) levels." Canonically, rs4680 GG = Val/Val = *higher* COMT activity = *faster* catecholamine breakdown; the literature on Val/Val and BP stress reactivity is mixed. **[HYPOTHESIS]** — could modulate sympathetic tone, but no strong claim is warranted given the source inconsistency.
- **CYP1A2 rs762551 CC (slow caffeine metabolizer)** — if she consumed coffee/tea during the Amlotel days, slower caffeine clearance would add sympathetic drive on top of the vasodilation. **[HYPOTHESIS]** — no intake data exist.
- **UGT2B15 TT** — the report notes a "40–50% decrease in removal of certain drugs" for this genotype; the affected drug list is not specified and neither amlodipine nor telmisartan is a glucuronidation-dependent drug. **[HYPOTHESIS/IRRELEVANT]** — unlikely to be the mechanism.
- **The panel never tested CYP3A4** (the primary amlodipine/ cilostazol/bromazepam pathway), **CYP2D6** (nebivolol, propafenone, and the old atenolol pathway), or **CYP2C19** (clopidogrel/cilostazol). So the pharmacogenetics that would matter for these drugs are simply absent — a gap that survives the correction and matters for the current regimen.

**Synthesis.** The amlodipine reflex tachycardia (resting 85, exertional 110–116) is a **normal dihydropyridine response** — the earlier "HR 185" was a typo (corrected 2026-08-05), so there is no extreme event for pharmacology or genetics to explain. The genetic profile neither incriminates nor clears her beta-receptor system, and the useful output is: (a) recognize beta-blocker withdrawal as a repeatable hazard (any future switch off nebivolol must be tapered); (b) with no documented abnormal rhythm, the 14-day rhythm patch is demoted to an **optional** consideration at the cardiologist's discretion (COMPLICATIONS.md §0.3, 2026-08-05); (c) consider CYP3A4/CYP2D6 testing if rhythm management with antiarrhythmics (e.g., the propafenone on the pharmacy label) is ever needed.

**Synthesis.** The overshoot confirms what §3.1 predicted — strong RAAS responsiveness — and validates the "start low, go slow" approach now in place. No new genetic action follows.

## 5. Data-Quality and Interpretation Cautions (Read Before Acting)

1. **The genetic report is a DTC nutrigenomics product, not a clinical genetic test.** It is explicitly informational; variants are common SNPs with small effect sizes; each finding is a probabilistic prior, not a diagnosis. Nothing in this analysis changes that.
2. **No biomarkers were measured in the genetic report.** It predicts elevated risk of high homocysteine, dysglycemia, low HDL, vitamin D/B12 deficiency, and estrogen burden — none of these were measured at any point in the clinical record (no homocysteine, no HbA1c, no 25-OH vitamin D, no ferritin, no estradiol). The correct use of this analysis is to *order the missing tests*, not to assume the risks are realized.
3. **Documented internal inconsistencies in the source report** (also flagged in GENETIC_REPORT.md), which cap how far any single variant can be leaned on:
   - **COMT rs4680 GG**: the report says Val/Val = "efficient estrogen metabolism, low estrogen, low dopamine" in one section, but "lower COMT enzyme activity and higher catecholamine levels" in the performance section. These cannot both be true. Canonically GG = Val/Val = *higher* enzyme activity. Treat the COMT narrative as unreliable in direction; keep only the weak claims (stress- and pain-modulation relevance).
   - **Summary-vs-detail label conflicts**: protein response ("Typical" vs "ENHANCED"), performance under pressure, atherosclerosis, oxidative stress, antioxidant-enzyme function, snacking.
   - **Conflicting genotypes for the same SNPs in different sections**: VDR rs1544410 appears as TT (calcium section) and CC (BMD section); rs731236 as GG and AA; rs2228570 as TC and AG. One of these calls is wrong in the source.
   - **Allele-naming oddity**: the pre-eclampsia section lists "MTHFR rs1801133 GG / rs1801131 GG," but these SNPs are C>T and A>C respectively; the methylation section's "CC/CC" is the internally consistent call. The pre-eclampsia "GG" naming is unreliable as printed.
4. **Pharmacogenetic coverage is incomplete in the most consequential places**: no CYP2C19, CYP2D6, CYP3A4, or SLCO1B1 anywhere in the panel. Absence of a finding ≠ absence of a risk. The clopidogrel question is genuinely open.
5. **Population-specificity**: some associations were established in European or East-Asian cohorts; effect sizes and even risk-allele directions can shift in Southeast-Asian populations (the report itself notes an Indonesia-specific TMPRSS6 effect). Treat effect sizes as indicative.
6. **Unmeasured lifestyle confounders**: no data on salt, caffeine, alcohol, sleep, or stress-exposure in the clinical file — exactly the variables that interact with the salt-sensitivity, caffeine, and COMT/DRD2/CLOCK findings. The conditional recommendations ("if she drinks coffee… if she sleeps poorly…") cannot be resolved from these records.
7. **The events and the sample are contemporaneous (July 2026)**; germline genotypes are fixed, so causality direction is safe, but the nutrigenomics panel was never designed to explain an acute hypertensive crisis — its value here is in *prioritization and mechanism*, not adjudication.
8. **Personal health information** — handle the combined file confidentially.
