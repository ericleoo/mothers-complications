## Executive Overview

In mid-July 2026, while attending a family funeral, a 55-year-old woman (Ratna Khouw) with a history of treated hypertension experienced a hypertensive crisis and an episode of progressive left-arm numbness — a transient ischemic attack (TIA)-type presentation — that resolved only after emergency intervention. Home readings showed blood pressure at 175/95 with a heart rate of 60 despite her standing regimen of atenolol 50 mg in the morning and irbesartan 300 mg at night. The numbness spread from the left upper arm to the palm (which turned cold while the right hand stayed warm), then migrated up the back of the left arm and into the neck while in the emergency department. Sublingual captopril brought the episode under control and the numbness resolved.

The follow-up — a trip to Kuching, Sarawak on July 15 and a full diagnostic assessment on July 16, 2026, followed by a medication overhaul and titration through late July — produced a mixed but actionable picture. The brain MRI with MRA found evidence of an **old lacunar infarct** in the left basal ganglia/internal capsule plus **moderate bilateral deep white-matter ischemic changes** consistent with cerebral small-vessel disease, with no large-vessel stenosis. The abdominal ultrasound found **gallstones** (largest 2.3 × 1.4 cm) with mildly elevated cholestatic liver enzymes (GGT 89, ALP 127, ALT 35) and normal bilirubin. Reassuringly, the exercise stress test was **normal at 11.1 METs** with no ischemic ECG changes, the chest radiograph was unremarkable, the lipid profile was optimal, thyroid function was normal, tumor markers were normal, and she has documented immunity to hepatitis B.

The Kuching physicians replaced the prior antihypertensive regimen with a single-combination tablet (amlodipine 10 mg + telmisartan 80 mg) plus an antiplatelet agent (clopidogrel 75 mg) and a vasodilator/antiplatelet (cilostazol 100 mg twice daily), with rosuvastatin 20 mg at night. The amlodipine component caused a dramatic reflex tachycardia — heart rate spiked to 185, and even minor exertion pushed it to 110 — followed by three nights of stomach pain and vomiting. A local physician, Dr. Budi Gunawan, discontinued the amlodipine, kept telmisartan 80 mg, added nebivolol (Nevodio) 5 mg for heart-rate control, and started ursodeoxycholic acid (Urdafalk) for one month to dissolve the gallstones, with a repeat ultrasound planned at the one-month mark.

The new regimen then overshot in the opposite direction — half a tablet of nebivolol plus full telmisartan produced 100/58 with dizziness — before the family settled on half a tablet of nebivolol morning and night without telmisartan, achieving 115/70 with a heart rate of 72. The most recent readings (July 31, 2026) show residual variability driven by activity and anxiety — 140/80 after housework, 137/78 after deep breathing, then 160/90 under stress — managed with telmisartan 80 mg and half a tablet of bromazepam (Akamon 3 mg) as needed. The final reading after a two-hour nap is not recorded in the source. Mama later confirmed (August 3, 2026, Part 7) that the telmisartan is exactly what makes her dizzy — her blood pressure drops too low — first-person confirmation of the rescue-telmisartan problem (read precisely: telmisartan 80 mg — the maximum dose — taken erratically; a dose/pattern finding, not a class verdict — see Part 7).

**The compounded anxiety medication ("obat cemas racik") — contents now documented.** The racikan contains exactly two ingredients:

The 5-tablet quantity implies a short, time-limited course. ## Insights & Implications

- **The amlodipine tachycardia episode illustrates a classic attribution trap — and the numbers don't fit the mechanism.** A high-dose dihydropyridine calcium-channel blocker (10 mg amlodipine) causes reflex sympathetic activation; that explains "even minor exertion pushed it to 110" (a 10–25 bpm reflex add). With a recorded peak exercise HR of 148 bpm (July 16, §2.5), a resting 185 is above her physiological sinus ceiling: **sinus tachycardia at 185 is effectively excluded on existing data**, and the episode should be treated as an undocumented arrhythmia until a 14-day rhythm patch says otherwise. - **The current regimen is now internally consistent with the Kuching report's recommendations.** The report called for a decision on secondary antiplatelet prophylaxis — effectively answered by starting clopidogrel plus cilostazol. The beta-blocker (nebivolol) controls both rate and pressure.
