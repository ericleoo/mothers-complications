## Core Content

### 1. Report Context, Science and How to Read Results

**Genetics and personalized medicine.** The report frames its rationale: genes contain the instructions to build thousands of proteins, each encoded by a sequence of DNA bases. Genetic variations can alter gene expression and thereby affect metabolic processes central to health. Understanding these variations enables personalized nutrition, lifestyle, and exercise guidance to optimize health, weight management, and performance.

**How to read results.** Each insight page follows a consistent template: an introductory explanation of the biological mechanism, a "Your Result" scale positioning the subject on a spectrum (e.g., from "No Increased Requirement" to "Increased Requirement," or "Typical" to "Elevated Risk"), a verbal "Outcome" with concrete actions, the specific gene variation and result, and a "GENE MARKERS" explanation of the relevant gene. Impact is graded on a six-point scale: No Impact/No Increase, Low Impact, Low to Moderate, Moderate, Moderate to High, and High Impact/Increase.

**Quality controls and sign-offs.** The report carries a disclaimer that it is for information only and not for diagnosis or treatment, notes the 99.75% concordance of the GeneTitan platform, and advises consulting a professional medical practitioner for follow-up. It is validated by the attending pathologist (Dr. Dr. Abas Suherli, Sp.PK(K)) and headed by the Head of Laboratory (Ridho Assidicky, M.Sc.).

**The two foundational prescriptions.**

- **Diet — Mediterranean Diet.** The recommended eating pattern is modeled on the traditional diets of Crete, Greece, and Southern Italy from the early 1960s, centered on olive cultivation — explicitly distinct from the modern "Westernized" eating patterns found in those regions today. The report states that research shows this diet is a powerful tool for preventing metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, certain neurological disorders, and even some cancers, and that it is highly effective for weight loss.
- **Exercise — 15.0 MET hours per week.** MET stands for Metabolic Equivalent Task, a unit that quantifies the energy expended during physical activity (watching TV ≈ 1.0 MET; running ≈ 8.0 METs). MET hours let one calculate time needed per week to hit the target. Based on the genetic results, the recommendation is **15.0 MET hours of physical activity per week**. The report advises starting slowly, increasing activity gradually, and consulting a doctor before starting a new exercise program.

### 3. Food Intolerances, Allergies and Sensitivity Traits

**Lactose Intolerance — Intolerant.** Caused by reduced lactase (the enzyme that breaks down milk sugar); symptoms (stomach upset, gas, bloating, diarrhea) can start within an hour of consuming lactose and are more common in adults. Variant: MCM6 rs4988235 CC (high impact). MCM6 regulates expression of the LCT gene (which encodes lactase); this variant reduces LCT expression. **Action:** Limit dairy milk intake if possible — no more than about one cup of milk per day; obtain calcium and vitamin D from lactose-free milk or fortified soy/almond milk; include tolerable dairy products; consult for symptom improvement and possible probiotic use.

**Gluten Intolerance (Coeliac Disease Risk) — High Risk.** Gluten (wheat, barley, rye) is problematic for those with coeliac disease (CD), an autoimmune disorder damaging the small intestine (symptoms: diarrhea, abdominal pain, weight loss, fatigue; ~1% of the population; requires lifelong gluten-free diet). Non-coeliac gluten sensitivity (NCGS) causes abdominal pain, diarrhea, fatigue, headaches after gluten. Variant: HLA **DQ2.5** (high impact). HLA-DQ2/DQ8 help the immune system recognize invaders; in coeliac disease these genes also recognize gluten and trigger an immune attack causing small-intestine inflammation. **Action:** If digestive symptoms occur, consider a gluten-free diet; gluten-free grains include quinoa and buckwheat; limit wheat, rye, oats, and barley; consult a nutritionist for guidance.

**Alcohol Metabolism — Slow Metabolizer.** Genetic variation in ALDH2 and environmental factors (alcohol volume) influence how quickly alcohol is broken down; alcohol's harm is tied to blood alcohol and the ability to clear the toxic byproduct acetaldehyde. Variant: ALDH2 rs671 AG (moderate). ALDH2 breaks down acetaldehyde; mutations lower enzyme activity, causing flushing, nausea, headache after drinking. **Action:** **Completely avoid alcohol.** Weight management is also important; limit intake of high-fat and high-saturated-fat foods (butter, lard, fast food).

**Caffeine Sensitivity: Bone Health — Typical.** High caffeine intake can influence calcium absorption and reduce bone mineral density, raising fracture risk. Variants: VDR rs731236 GG, rs1544410 TT, rs2228570 TC. **Action:** Follow typical/health-conscious caffeine intake guidelines.

**Caffeine Sensitivity: Anxiety and Sleep — Typical.** Caffeine blocks adenosine (promoting alertness) but can cause anxiety in some genotypes. Variant: COMT rs4680 GG. **Action:** Follow typical healthy recommendations for tea, coffee, and other caffeinated beverages.

**Caffeine Sensitivity: Performance — Non-Responder.** Moderate caffeine can enhance sprint and endurance performance, but response depends on metabolizer type and habitual intake. Variant: CYP1A2 rs762551 CC (high). CYP1A2 metabolizes caffeine; slow metabolizers feel caffeine's effects longer and are more sensitive. **Action:** Caffeine intake won't improve performance. Restrict coffee to fewer than three cups daily or choose decaffeinated.

**Caffeine Sensitivity: Heart Health — Sensitive.** Coffee's cardiovascular impact hinges on the CYP1A2 variant (fast vs slow metabolizer); caffeine can prompt blood-pressure surges and heart-disease vulnerability in slow metabolizers, who also show higher risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes. Variant: CYP1A2 rs762551 CC (high). **Action:** Restrict caffeinated beverages to **3 cups a day at maximum**, or choose decaffeinated.

**Salt Sensitivity — Sensitive.** Salt sensitivity affects how blood pressure responds to sodium; sodium is often overconsumed, and excess raises blood pressure, contributing to hypertension, heart disease, and stroke; the ACE gene is involved. Variants: AGT rs699 CC (high), ACE rs4341 GG (no impact). ACE converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II (a vasoconstrictor); AGT is the precursor to angiotensin I; certain variations raise the likelihood of high blood pressure on a high-sodium diet. **Action:** Reduce salt intake; if you have hypertension, decreasing salt consumption is advantageous.

**Peanut Allergy — Elevated Risk.** Peanut allergy can be life-threatening (reactions from itching/swelling to anaphylaxis); genetics influence immune sensitivity to nut proteins. Variants: Intergenic rs9275596 TC (high), SKAP1 rs72827854 CC (low). **Action:** Peanut allergy accounts for a large share (an estimated ~70–90%) of fatalities from food-induced allergy, including anaphylaxis; although nut-allergy prevalence is typically low in Asia, given the severity avoid peanut-containing foods if at risk.

**Egg Allergy — Elevated Risk.** Egg allergy is a common immune overreaction to egg proteins (skin rash, stomach upset, or trouble breathing in severe cases). Variant: SERPINB7/B2 rs1243064 AT (high). **Action:** In most Asian and Southeast Asian countries, egg allergy is more common than cow's-milk allergy (prevalence in China up to ~4% vs. ~1–1.6% in Western populations). If no allergic symptoms occur when eating eggs, it may reflect desensitization from oral immunotherapy (OIT) — the gradual introduction of increasing egg-protein doses to reduce sensitivity. Avoid egg intake if possible, or consider oral immunotherapy under a doctor's supervision.

3. **A recurring "inflammation + methylation + detox" theme.** Elevated risk of chronic low-grade inflammation (IL-1 family, TNFA, IL-6), elevated homocysteine/methylation risk (MTHFR, MTR, CBS, COMT), and reduced phase-2 detoxification (GSTM1, GSTT1, GSTP1, NQO1) converge. 10. **Overall the profile is mixed but leans toward modifiable lifestyle levers** — the elevated risks are largely addressable through the Mediterranean diet, exercise, stress/sleep management, and specific food choices rather than through unlikely drugs.

## Insights & Implications

**The "inflammation/estrogen/methylation/detox axis."** Many superficially unrelated insights trace back to the same four biological systems. Managing any one (e.g., lowering inflammation) tends to support the others, which means the Mediterranean diet + anti-inflammatory eating + folate/choline/B vitamins + stress/sleep management is a high-leverage, mutually reinforcing package rather than a list of disconnected rules. The homocysteine and estrogen findings both counsel against alcohol and support fiber-rich, plant-forward eating.

**A personalized wrinkle on the standard advice.** The report's value is largely in *direction and prioritization*: caffeine won't help performance and its heart effect is adverse for this CYP1A2 genotype; alcohol should be avoided outright (ALDH2); vitamin E should come from food, not supplements; whole grains and omega-6/omega-3 balance are emphasized over blanket low-fat advice because of the HDL and diabetes findings. It is a reminder that "healthy defaults" can be sharpened by genotype.
